Working with a travel advisor

What does it cost to work with The Motley Guide?

I don’t charge you a planning or service fee for a cruise booking. When you book, I’m paid directly by the travel partner. That compensation comes from the partner, not as an added fee to you.

Does booking through you cost more than booking direct?

Great question. No—booking through me doesn’t cost more than booking direct. I can access the same publicly available cruise fares and inventory you see online, and I’ll also check for eligible promotions. Cruise prices and offers can change based on the sailing, cabin, dates, and availability, so I’ll walk you through the current options before you book.

Every booking earns travel rewards. One of the best parts of working with a travel advisor is having someone keep an eye on your cruise fare for price drops. If the cruise line’s rules and your fare type allow an adjustment, I can help you explore it.

How are you paid if your service is free to me?

Travel partners compensate me when I help arrange a booking. You pay the travel partner for your trip under that partner’s payment terms; my planning service doesn’t add a separate fee to your cruise fare.

Do I receive travel rewards when I book with you?

Yes. I offer travel rewards with every booking I handle. The reward and how it is delivered can vary by trip and travel partner. I’ll share the reward details with you before you book so you know what to expect.

Planning your cruise

What information should I have ready?

A few basics help me get started: your preferred destination or region, approximate travel dates, number of travelers, cabin preferences, and a comfortable budget range. If you’re flexible, that’s useful too.

Can you help me compare cruise lines and cabins?

Yes. I can help compare itineraries, ships, cabin categories, and available offers based on what matters most to you. I’ll make recommendations, then you choose what feels right.

Can you book Virgin Voyages?

Yes. You can explore and book Virgin Voyages through my First Mate link. If you’d like help choosing a voyage or cabin, use one of the Email Edward buttons on this site and share your preferences with me.

Who takes my payment, and when is it due?

As your travel advisor, I process your payments on your behalf with the respective travel partner. For your card information, I can send you a PCI-compliant invoice, or call you to collect it—whichever you’re more comfortable with.

With most cruise lines, the final payment is due at least 120 days before departure. The exact deadline depends on the cruise line, sailing, and when you book. If you book closer to departure, payment may be due at booking. I’ll confirm the payment schedule with you before the booking is completed.

What if I need to change or cancel my cruise?

Change and cancellation terms are set by the cruise line or travel partner and may depend on the sailing and timing. I can help you understand the applicable terms and assist with next steps, while any fees or refunds follow the partner’s policies.

Can you help with excursions, hotels, or travel insurance?

Yes. I can also help you look at pre- or post-cruise hotel stays, excursions, and travel protection options, depending on your itinerary and needs.

How far in advance should I book my cruise?

Booking six to twelve months in advance is a good starting point, especially for popular destinations, holiday sailings, and specific stateroom categories. Specialty cruises and highly anticipated itineraries may benefit from booking even earlier.

Do I have to pay for my entire cruise when booking?

Not always! Many cruise lines allow you to secure your reservation with an initial deposit, with the remaining balance due before sailing.

Some promotional fares require full payment or have nonrefundable deposits. Payment terms vary by cruise line, fare type, and departure date.

Before you sail

Do I need a passport for my cruise?

Travel-document rules depend on your citizenship, itinerary, ports, and cruise line. Some eligible sailings may allow U.S. citizens to use alternative documents, but a valid passport book can offer more flexibility if an emergency requires international air travel. Check the exact requirements for every traveler and sailing before departure.

How long should my passport be valid, and do I need a visa?

Some destinations require a passport to remain valid for a period beyond your trip, and visa or electronic travel authorization rules vary by citizenship and itinerary. Confirm current requirements with the appropriate government authorities and cruise line well before you travel.

Are there age requirements for booking or sailing?

Minimum ages for infants, unaccompanied guests, and guests sharing a stateroom vary by cruise line, itinerary, and departure location. Some voyages are adults-only. I can help identify the policy that applies to the trip you’re considering; the cruise line’s current rules control.

When should I arrive in the cruise departure city?

If you’re flying to the port, I recommend arriving at least one day before embarkation when possible. That gives you a buffer for delays or cancellations. Keep your travel documents, medications, valuables, and essentials in your carry-on rather than checked luggage.

Cruising With Children & Age Requirements

How old must I be to book a cruise stateroom?

Age requirements vary by cruise line and departure location. Many major cruise lines require guests to be at least 21 years old to sail unaccompanied from North American ports, although exceptions exist.

For example:

Cruise lineGeneral North American sailing policy
Royal Caribbean21+ to sail unaccompanied; exceptions apply
Norwegian Cruise LineGuests under 21 generally need an accompanying guest aged 21+ in the same, connecting, or adjoining stateroom
Carnival Cruise LineGuests 20 and younger generally need a relative or guardian aged 25+; exceptions apply
Virgin VoyagesAdults-only cruises; guests must be at least 18

These are simplified examples, not complete stateroom assignment rules. Policies can change and exceptions may apply, so confirm the current terms for your exact sailing before booking.

Can my children stay in their own stateroom?

Sometimes! Cruise lines may allow children or teenagers to occupy separate staterooms when traveling with parents or guardians, subject to age, proximity, supervision, and booking requirements.

Connecting rooms, adjoining rooms, and rooms directly across the hallway may be treated differently. Always confirm the cruise line's specific policy before booking.

What is the minimum age for a baby to cruise?

Many cruise lines require infants to be at least six months old on embarkation day. Certain longer itineraries, transatlantic crossings, and voyages with consecutive sea days may require infants to be at least 12 months old.

The minimum age depends on the cruise line and itinerary.

Can children cruise with only one parent or with grandparents?

Yes, in many cases. However, additional documentation may be required, particularly when a child travels without both legal parents or guardians.

Depending on the cruise line and destinations, a notarized parental consent letter, proof of guardianship, or other documentation may be needed. Check the current requirements before departure.

Are there activities for children and teenagers onboard?

Absolutely! Many family-friendly cruise lines offer supervised youth programs, teen clubs, splash areas, family entertainment, and age-appropriate activities.

Program availability, age limits, registration requirements, and operating hours vary by ship.

Are all cruise lines family-friendly?

No. Some cruise lines cater specifically to adults. For example, Virgin Voyages is an adults-only cruise line requiring guests to be at least 18 years old.

The Motley Guide can help you select a cruise line that best matches your family, travel style, and vacation expectations.

Travel Insurance & Protecting Your Vacation

Is travel insurance required for a cruise?

Travel insurance is not universally required, but certain cruise lines, destinations, or itineraries may require specific coverage.

The Motley Guide highly recommends purchasing travel insurance to help protect your vacation investment against eligible unexpected events.

What does travel insurance typically cover?

Depending on the plan, travel insurance may provide benefits for trip cancellation or interruption, medical emergencies, emergency medical evacuation, travel delays, missed connections, and lost or delayed baggage.

Every policy has exclusions, coverage limits, and eligibility requirements. Review your policy carefully before purchasing.

Does my regular health insurance cover me while cruising?

Not necessarily. Many health insurance policies provide limited or no coverage outside the United States or aboard cruise ships. Medicare generally does not cover medical care outside the U.S., except in limited circumstances.

We recommend confirming your existing coverage and considering travel medical and emergency evacuation insurance. Review Medicare’s travel coverage guidance ↗

Can I purchase travel insurance after booking my cruise?

Often, yes! However, purchasing coverage soon after your initial trip deposit may help you qualify for certain time-sensitive benefits, such as pre-existing medical condition waivers or optional Cancel For Any Reason coverage, where available. Availability, deadlines, and eligibility vary by plan.

Can I decline travel insurance?

Yes, when insurance is optional. The Motley Guide strongly recommends protecting your trip, but the decision is ultimately yours. Travelers who decline coverage accept the financial risks of traveling without it.

Onboard & in port

What is usually included in a cruise fare?

Many fares include your stateroom, selected dining, entertainment, and access to some ship amenities. Specialty dining, drinks, Wi-Fi, gratuities, spa services, and shore excursions may cost extra or depend on the fare package. I’ll help you review what is included in the specific option you’re considering.

Can I book excursions outside the cruise line?

Often, travelers can explore independently or book with an outside tour operator, depending on the port and activity. With an independent excursion, you are responsible for returning to the ship on time, and the ship may not wait if you are late. Review the excursion details and the ship’s all-aboard time carefully.

Can I cruise with medical, dietary, or accessibility needs?

Options and accommodations vary by ship, port, and excursion. Share any accessibility, mobility, dietary, or other needs as early as possible so we can review what may be available. For medical questions, consult your healthcare provider and confirm the ship’s capabilities directly with the cruise line.

Will my phone work at sea?

Your carrier may charge maritime or international roaming rates while you’re at sea or in port. Check with your mobile provider before travel, review the ship’s Wi-Fi options, and consider airplane mode to avoid unexpected charges.

Hotels & all-inclusive resorts

Can you help me book a hotel or all-inclusive resort?

Yes. I can help you find hotel stays and all-inclusive resorts that fit your destination, dates, travel style, and budget. I can also help coordinate a hotel before or after a cruise.

What does “all-inclusive” include?

Inclusions vary by resort and room plan. Meals, drinks, activities, transfers, gratuities, and premium experiences may be handled differently from one property to another. I’ll help review what is included in the option you’re considering and point out any additional costs.

Are hotel and resort bookings also free to plan?

I don’t charge a separate planning fee for hotel or all-inclusive resort bookings. The travel partner pays me directly when I arrange a booking. I also offer travel rewards with every booking I handle, and I’ll explain the reward details before you book.

Who sets hotel payment and cancellation terms?

The hotel, resort, or travel partner sets the deposit, payment, change, and cancellation terms. These can differ by property, rate, and booking date. I’ll help you review the terms before you decide.

Flights

Can you book my flights?

I don’t book flights. If a delay, cancellation, or other travel interruption happens, booking airfare through an intermediary can add a step: the airline may direct ticket changes back through the original booking channel, which can make it harder to resolve things directly with customer service at the airport and create extra logistical challenges.

I recommend booking flights directly with the airline so you can work with its customer service team yourself if plans are disrupted.
